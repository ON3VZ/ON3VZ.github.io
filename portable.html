HF · 25 W

Portable HF station

The same radio as the shack, deployed in the field from a LiFePO4 battery, with a resonant wire antenna for quick WWFF setups.

Transceiver · HF / 6 m

ICOM IC-7300 MK II

The shack transceiver doubles as the portable rig. Compact, with a low receive current of about 0.7 A, and the spectrum scope finds activity fast in the field. Runs 25 W on Class C.

Field power

LiFePO4 · 50 Ah

A lithium iron phosphate battery: a flat voltage curve near 13.2 V until almost empty, more than 2000 cycles and a safe chemistry. At 25 W and a typical WWFF duty cycle, one charge lasts a full day.

Antennas

Two wire antennas

SOTAbeams Band Hopper IV. Linked dipole for 80, 40, 30 and 20 m, full size and resonant on every band, no tuner needed.

Homebrew 40 m dipole. A simple monoband wire dipole for 40 m, built at home.

QRP · ON3VZ/P

5 watts, on foot

A second, much smaller station built for WWFF and ONFF activations. Everything fits in a backpack, and it runs under its own callsign, ON3VZ/P.

Transceiver · HF QRP

QRP Labs QMX

A five-band QRP transceiver about the size of a pack of cards, 220 g with its enclosure, for CW, digital modes and SSB at 5 W. One USB-C cable carries both CAT control and audio.

It runs from a LiFePO4 pack regulated down to 11.50 V by an adjustable buck converter, because the QMX accepts 6.0 to 12.0 V only.

  • Bands80, 60, 40, 30, 20 m

Antenna and mast

Band Hopper IV on a Tactical 7000hds

The SOTAbeams Band Hopper IV runs as an inverted V on the lightweight fibreglass mast. The 40, 30 and 20 m links do the work: full size, no tuner and no loading coils. The 80 m section stays on its winder.

The dipole legs double as guys for the mast, so both sides need the same tension. And the golden rule: the same number of links closed on both legs, counted on both sides.

Digital transceiver

QRP Labs QDX

A 5-band digital-modes transceiver at 5 W, made for FT8 and the other digital modes. A small, dedicated companion next to the QMX.

A separate identity

ON3VZ/P

ON3VZ/P has its own QRZ.com logbook, LoTW certificate and Log4OM configuration, kept apart from the shack log. QRP is shown through the power field, spot comments and the profile, not through a callsign suffix.

Handheld

Dual-band handheld

Yaesu FT-65

A rugged 2 m and 70 cm handheld for WWFF and outdoor walk-around use, and the backup for the FTM-510DE ASP in the car. Here on 144.675 MHz, the WLD club frequency.

With an SMA to PL-259 pigtail it connects straight to the Komunica magnet mount: a full-size antenna with handheld convenience.

NETWORK

SvxLink in the pocket

The Belgian SVX Reflector network links repeaters and hotspots across the country. With the app on my smartphone and a Bluetooth speaker microphone, it works just like a handheld, wherever there is mobile data.

How it works

App, talkgroup, PTT

The app connects to the reflector as ON3VZ-D. I pick a talkgroup, for example TG 8 for the 70 cm repeaters, and press PTT on the screen or on the microphone.

The ABBREE Bluetooth speaker microphone pairs with the phone, so the phone stays in the pocket while the mic clips onto a jacket or a bag strap.
