Sometimes everything lines up. On Monday 28 September 2026, in the middle of the afternoon here in Hoboken, I made an SSB contact on 15 metres with **Hermanto (Herman), YC7ONI**, in Indonesia. He was on Borneo, more than 11,000 km away. I was running just **25 watts**, he was running 100 watts. WOW!

## Not luck, but preparation

This contact did not just fall out of the sky. It was less than a week after the autumn equinox, traditionally the best time of year for DX on the higher HF bands. Before switching on, I worked it out for myself: the sunspot number, the predicted MUF and the reliability of the path to South-East Asia, hour by hour. From that I picked my moment.

The prediction gave a MUF of just over 22 MHz. That put 15 metres just below the limit of what the ionosphere would still bend back to earth, which is exactly where you want to be.

Even so, the software gave the path only a **35 % chance** of being open at that hour. On paper a marginal path. But marginal is not impossible.

## Keep listening

So I kept listening. Herman's signal was above the noise, but at first there was a lot of QSB: it kept fading in and out. Then it grew stronger and stronger. He was working other stations, and I followed those QSOs until I could understand them, more or less. That is the moment you sit up straight as a radio amateur: now or never. I took my chance, called, and he came back to me!

Reports: **53 sent, 53 received**. Not a strong signal, and hard work to copy through the fading, but readable. There is a reason the comment in my log says "crazy propagation".

## How was this possible with 25 watts?

**1. The frequency sat just below the MUF.** My frequency was at about 96 % of the predicted MUF. There, absorption in the low D layer is minimal and the signal is reflected at a low angle. Long hops mean fewer hops, and every hop you save is loss you do not suffer. Over 11,450 km there were still at least three.

**2. The equinox.** Around the equinox both hemispheres receive the same amount of sunlight. That gives the best conditions of the year for 15 metres, especially on paths heading towards the equator.

**3. Timing along the path.** Here it was mid-afternoon. The midpoint of the path, over the Karakoram and the western Himalaya, had just passed sunset, and on Borneo it was already after nine in the evening. The eastern half of the path was in darkness. There the absorbing D layer disappears quickly, while the higher F2 layer stays active for a long time.

**4. The equatorial effect.** Borneo lies right on the equator. After sunset the F layer there gets an extra lift and ionisation stays high well into the evening. That effect is strongest in the hours after local sunset, which is exactly when my contact happened.

**5. No polar crossing.** The path stays far south of the polar regions. So there is no auroral absorption, unlike paths to Japan or the US West Coast.

**6. The antenna.** The feedpoint of my HyEndFed sloper sits at about 10 metres, above the flat roof and above the surrounding buildings. The low take-off angles that such a long path needs can leave freely. On 15 metres the wire has distinct lobes, and towards South-East Asia I am clearly in a good one. The sloper also picks up less local noise than a vertical in a city garden. With weak signals, that is the difference between "just not" and "readable".

**7. Power matters less than you think.** 25 watts against 100 watts is a difference of 6 dB, or about one S-unit. The path loss is the same in both directions. With the right conditions and a decent antenna, that one S-unit does not decide the contact.

## What I take away from this

This does not always work, and signals will not always be this kind. The prediction itself said the path opens on roughly one day in three. But if you follow the conditions, pick your moment, keep listening and have a reasonable antenna, you can reach the other side of the world from a city garden in Hoboken with modest power.

Reasoning out the timing myself and then hearing it actually happen, even with a fading signal that took real effort to copy, made this one extra special. I am over the moon with it. This is one of those contacts you do this hobby for. Terima kasih, Herman (YC7ONI), and 73!
