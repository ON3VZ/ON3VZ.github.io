## 3. How to check in, and Belgian callsigns {#fn-3}

<div class="dm-box dm-steps" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Step plan: your first check-in</p>

1. Tune in five minutes early and only listen. Every net leader has his own rhythm.
2. Wait until the net leader asks for check-ins ("stations for the net, please call").
3. Say your callsign once, clearly, in the phonetic alphabet: "Oscar November Three Victor Zulu". Then release the PTT.
4. The net leader repeats your callsign and adds you to the list. When it is your turn, give your name, your location and a signal report.
5. Keep it short and hand back: "back to net control".
6. Leaving early? Say so, so you can be struck from the list.
</div>

You will hear a lot of callsigns. A few things that help:

- A Belgian callsign starts with **ON**, then a digit, then two or three letters. Mine is ON3VZ, a basic licence (Class C). The UBA explains how callsigns are built on its [callsigns and QSL cards page](https://www.uba.be/nl/info/roepnamen-qsl-kaarten) (in Dutch).
- Calls starting with **OO, OP, OR, OS or OT** are often special event or contest calls, for example OT5A (the Lier contest station) or OS90OSA (the 90th anniversary of the Antwerp section).
- UBA members belong to a **section** with a three-letter code (OSA, WLD, KSD ...). Nets and contests use it, for example in an ON contest you send "59001WLD". The [list of sections](https://www.uba.be/en/contact/districts) shows where each one lives.
- A callsign followed by **/P** is portable, **/M** mobile and **/AM** aeronautical mobile.

## 4. FM: simplex and repeater {#fn-4}

<div class="dm-box" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Situation</p>

**Simplex** (most club nets, for example ONZ on 145.475): everybody uses the same frequency, no offset, usually no tone. You hear the other stations directly, so what you hear depends on distance.

**Repeater** (for example OSA on ON0DP): the repeater listens on one frequency and transmits on another. On your radio the **receive** frequency is the repeater's output, the **transmit** frequency its input (here 0.6 MHz lower), and you must send the **CTCSS tone** (131.8 Hz). Without the tone the repeater stays closed for you.
</div>

Tip for your radio: make one channel per net and name it after the club ("OSA RONDE", "KSD RONDE"). Then you do not have to type a frequency at 19:59. How I build that in a DM-1801 Pro is in [Part 1]({% post_url 2026-10-29-dm1801pro-part-1-setup %}) and [Part 2]({% post_url 2026-10-29-dm1801pro-part-2-daily-use %}).

## 5. SvxLink: three ways in {#fn-5}

SvxLink links repeaters (and some simplex gateways) over the internet. A call on one is heard on all. The live overview is at [portal.be.svx.link](https://portal.be.svx.link/), and the [CQ België page](https://cqbelgie.radio-actief.be/svx-link-netwerk-vlaanderen/) explains the network.

<div class="dm-box dm-steps" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Three ways to join</p>

1. **Via a repeater.** All SvxLink repeaters in Flanders use a shift of -7.6 MHz. You do not program anything special: choose the nearest one, send the right subtone (79.7 or 131.8 Hz, see the table below), press PTT and call. You are heard on all linked repeaters.
2. **Via a simplex gateway.** The easiest example is KSD: 145.425 MHz FM simplex, no CTCSS, linked through a gateway on the IJzer tower in Diksmuide (SvxLink TG 8670). The Tuesday net of KSD is therefore reachable far beyond Koksijde.
3. **Via an app or an analog hotspot.** SvxLink clients exist for phones, for example [Latry](https://latry-app-landing.pages.dev/) (Android and iOS). You enter the reflector host, port, your callsign, an authentication key and a talkgroup. I have not set this up yet **(to be tested)**; you need credentials from the network, so start at the portal. RF.Guru also sells an analog SvxLink hotspot.
</div>

## 6. DMR: get the net on your radio automatically {#fn-6}

Three settings must match or you hear nothing: **colour code 1** (Flanders), the right **timeslot**, and the **talkgroup in your RX group list** (or equal to the channel's TX contact). Both DMR nets in the planner use an on-demand talkgroup on **timeslot 2**: the MLB net on TG 2067 and the NOK net on TG 2069. In the BrandMeister wiki, [TG 2064 to 2069 are the on-demand groups of Belgium, on slot 2](https://wiki.brandmeister.network/index.php/Belgium).

<div class="dm-box dm-why" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Why you must "call it in" first</p>

On-demand groups are **dynamic**. A repeater or hotspot only carries one after somebody keys up on it, and it [drops again after about 15 minutes without traffic](https://help.brandmeister.network/talk-groups/dynamic-vs-static/). Static groups are always on. So for a net on TG 2069 you either activate the group yourself a few minutes before the start, or you let the network do it for you (see "Hotspot").
</div>

<div class="dm-box dm-steps" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Step plan: via a repeater (for example ON0NOK, 438.7125 MHz, CC1)</p>

1. Make sure the talkgroup (2069 for NOK, 2067 for MLB) is in the **RX group list** of the channel you will use.
2. Select a channel with **TS2** and **TX contact = the net's talkgroup**. On my DM-1801 Pro that is the QSY channel of the repeater with a manual dial, or the zone QSY QTH DMR, as explained in [Part 2]({% post_url 2026-10-29-dm1801pro-part-2-daily-use %}).
3. A few minutes before the start, key up briefly and give your callsign. The repeater now carries the group.
4. During a long pause the group can drop. Key up briefly again to bring it back.
</div>

<div class="dm-box dm-steps" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Step plan: via your own hotspot (the easiest)</p>

1. In my codeplug the zone **Hotspot** already has the channels HS 2067 and HS 2069 (438.100 MHz, CC1, TS2), and the RX group list **RX Hotspot** contains both groups. See [Part 1]({% post_url 2026-10-29-dm1801pro-part-1-setup %}).
2. Simplex hotspot: the last group you key up becomes **auto-static**. [It behaves like a dynamic group, except that it does not time out](https://amateurradionotes.com/auto-static.htm), even if you are not transmitting. Key up once on TG 2069 and the net comes in.
3. Done? Send TG 4000 to disconnect.
4. Fully automatic: BrandMeister SelfCare has **Scheduled Static**. Open your hotspot in SelfCare, scroll to Scheduled Static, click **Add Scheduled Static**, type the talkgroup, choose the day, pick a three-hour block and the start and stop date. Example: TG 2067, Wednesday, 19:30 to 22:30. Both UTC and local time are shown, so watch the switch between summer and winter time. **(To be tested on my hotspot.)**
</div>

<div class="dm-box dm-steps" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Step plan: without a radio or hotspot</p>

1. Install DroidStar and set it up as in [Part 3]({% post_url 2026-11-03-dmr-on-the-smartphone-droidstar %}).
2. Select TG 2067 or 2069 and connect before the net starts.
3. Just listening? The Hoseline plays any talkgroup in a browser: [TG 2067](https://hose.brandmeister.network/?subscribe=2067) and [TG 2069](https://hose.brandmeister.network/?subscribe=2069).
</div>

<div class="dm-box dm-check" markdown="1">
<p class="dm-box-label">Checklist: I hear nothing</p>

- RX frequency is the repeater's **output**, TX its **input**, and the colour code is **1**.
- Timeslot 2 for the on-demand groups.
- The talkgroup is in the RX group list of the channel.
- Nobody has keyed the group up yet: key up briefly yourself.
- TX works but nobody answers: check the TX contact of the channel, because an answer later than the hold time goes out on your channel's own talkgroup.
</div>

## 7. 80 m: a word for ON3 {#fn-7}

Only one confirmed net in the planner uses 80 m: the Grimbergen net on every other Wednesday, around 3.700 MHz with some QRM. As an ON3 I may only use part of each band, so look at the [UBA band plan](https://www.uba.be/en/hf/bandplanning) and the licence conditions before you call on HF. On the net itself the same check-in steps apply.
