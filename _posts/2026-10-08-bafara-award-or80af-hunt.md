The Belgian Air Force is celebrating its 80th anniversary, and the Belgian Air Force Amateur Radio Association (BAFARA) marks it with the BAFARA Award OR80AF, running from 1 to 31 October 2026. The activity was forwarded to us at my radio club WLD for the award hunters among us, and with my long-standing love for aviation I was hooked straight away.

## How the scoring works

A contact counts once per station, band and mode, whatever the date. A second contact with the same station on the same band in the same mode adds nothing. Modes are grouped into CW, phone and digital, and what a contact is worth depends on the kind of station:

| Station | CW | Phone | Digital |
|---|---|---|---|
| OR80AF (HQ) | 10 | 10 | 5 |
| Air base station | 5 | 5 | 3 |
| Member station | 2 | 2 | 1 |

The grades are **Solo** at 25 points, **Wingman** at 50, **Leader** at 75 and **Instructor** at 100. Stations upload their logs after the fact, so contacts only show up in the official standings a day or a few days later. The point scale was still waiting for committee approval when I wrote this, so it may change.

## My hunting plan

I work this from my Class C station on 25 W, on the bands I am allowed to use: 80, 40, 30, 20, 15 and 10 m, in every mode. There is no phone on 30 m. The HQ station is worth the most on every band, so it is the first target, then the air base stations, then the member stations.

Because only the first contact per combination counts, the real work is bookkeeping: which station, on which band, in which mode, do I still need? That is what the tracker answers.

## Follow along

The live tracker shows every station, band and mode as a jet that turns from grey to green once it is in my log, plus my score and how far I am from the next grade. You find it under **Trackers** in the menu, or go straight to the [OR80AF tracker](/trackers/or80af/). After 31 October it stays online as a record of my final result.
