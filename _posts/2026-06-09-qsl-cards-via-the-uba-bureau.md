## 1. What a QSL Card Is, and the Three Ways to Exchange One {#what}

A **QSL card** is the written confirmation of a radio contact (a *QSO*). The code "QSL" comes from the international Q-code and means "I confirm receipt". In practice it is a kind of postcard that proves, in black and white, that two stations actually worked (or heard) each other. It has been called the "final courtesy of a QSO" since the earliest days of radio: you send your card, the other station sends one back, and only when both have been exchanged is the contact fully confirmed.

What is it good for? As a **keepsake** and lasting proof of a special contact, as **evidence for awards** such as DXCC, WAC, WAS and the UBA diplomas, and for **SWL listeners (ONL)** as confirmation of a reception report.

There are roughly three ways to exchange QSLs:

| Channel | Speed | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Via the bureau** | Slow (months, sometimes over a year) | Included in your UBA membership | The cheapest paper route. This is what this post is about. |
| **Direct** | Fast (weeks) | Postage, roughly €1 to €3 per card | By post to the other station, with return postage. |
| **Electronic** | Minutes to days | Free or subscription | LoTW, eQSL, QRZ.com, Club Log. No paper, but counts for most awards (see my [digital guide](/2026/05/06/from-first-qso-to-first-qsl-card/)). |

> 💡 **In short:** the UBA bureau is by far the cheapest way to send paper cards. You pay nothing extra: the cost sits in your UBA membership. The trade-off is patience. Reckon on months, sometimes more than a year, before a reply card comes back.

## 2. May You Use the Bureau? {#eligible}

The QSL service is a member service. To use it, a few simple conditions apply:

- You are a **UBA member** affiliated with a section. For me that is **WLD (ON6WL)**. You hand in your cards at, and receive them through, that section.
- The cost of the service is paid from a part of your membership fee, so you pay nothing per card.
- Make sure the membership administration has your correct details (callsign, address, email). Something changed? Report it via [members@uba.be](mailto:members@uba.be).
- If you ever operate under a holiday, expedition or foreign callsign, that callsign must be registered separately with the membership administration (with a copy of the licence if it concerns a non-CEPT country). Otherwise incoming cards for that call cannot reach you.

> ⚠️ **Heads up:** non-members cannot use the bureau. Hand in only your own cards, and never pass cards from a non-member through the section, as those are refused.

## 3. How to Fill Out a Card {#fillout}

Every card needs the **details of the QSO**. Without them the other station cannot find your contact in their log, and the card is worthless. Here is what belongs on a card, and the layout rules that keep the sorter happy.

<div class="img-full">
  <img src="/assets/images/qsl-card-anatomy.svg" alt="Annotated QSL card showing the mandatory fields: callsign, addressee call, UTC time, band, mode, RST and QSL via" loading="lazy">
</div>

### The mandatory fields

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| **Counterpart callsign** | Who the card is addressed to. Place it prominently. |
| **Date** | The date of the QSO, written unambiguously (e.g. 09-jun-2026). |
| **Time in UTC** | Always UTC, never local time. This is the most common beginner mistake. |
| **Band or frequency** | For example 20 m or 14.074 MHz. |
| **Mode** | SSB, CW, FT8, RTTY, and so on. |
| **RST report** | The signal report you gave (e.g. 59 or 599). |
| **PSE / TNX QSL** | "PSE QSL" asks for a card back, "TNX QSL" thanks for one received. |
| **Your own details** | Your callsign (ON3VZ), name, QTH and locator (JO21EE), optionally rig, antenna and power. |

### Layout rules for the bureau

**Do this**, it makes the sorter's work easy: put the addressee's callsign clearly and large (that is what gets sorted), keep the card tidy and readable, and use the word **"VIA"** when the card needs to go to a QSL manager (see [section 4](#via)).

**Avoid this**, it causes errors or delay: never print the **address of the UBA bureau** on your card (the foreign bureaus already know it, and it can change), never place two callsigns right next to or under each other without making clear who the card is for (an ONL card usually carries two calls), and avoid tiny or sloppy text that the sorter has to decipher with a magnifying glass.

### Form, size and weight

Stick to the IARU standard size: **90 × 140 mm**, on **190 to 250 g/m²** card stock. That makes sorting and bundling smoother.

> 🥇 **Golden tip for newcomers:** note the time in UTC, not local time. If you write "21:00" meaning 21:00 Belgian time, the other station will not find your QSO (in their log it sits at 19:00 or 20:00 UTC). One wrong time equals no confirmation.

## 4. The Scenarios: What Does "QSL VIA" Mean? {#via}

Not every card goes straight to the other station. Here are the situations you will meet as a beginner, and what to do each time.

| Situation | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **You operate from abroad** (e.g. F/ON3VZ) | Cards for your holiday or foreign call belong to you at home. | Nothing special: they come back to you via the bureau. No "via" needed. |
| **Counterpart has a QSL manager** (e.g. J8LP via K3LP) | The DX station has someone else handle their cards. | Write "J8LP via K3LP" and sort on the **manager's** bureau (here the USA). Never "c/o", and never reversed ("K3LP via J8LP"). |
| **Belgian station via Belgian station** (ON4yyy via ON4xxx) | A Belgian amateur routes their cards via another Belgian. | Allowed only if both are UBA members. |
| **You manage a foreign call** (e.g. 5X1T via ON3VZ) | You want to receive cards for a non-Belgian call. | That call must be registered with the membership administration (with a licence copy), or you provide proof it is the same person. |
| **DX without a bureau, no manager** | There is no IARU bureau in the other station's country. | Always ask "QSL via?" during the QSO. No manager? Then send direct to the given address (often a P.O. Box). |

> 📌 **Remember about "VIA":** always use the word **VIA** to indicate where the card should go, and decide the bureau based on the **manager**, not the DX station. So "J8LP via K3LP" goes to the bureau of the USA (K = USA).

## 5. The Journey of Your Card {#journey}

Once you hand your cards in, they follow a fixed route. Since the 2026 reorganisation it looks like this.

<div class="img-full">
  <img src="/assets/images/qsl-bureau-route.svg" alt="Diagram of the outgoing and incoming UBA bureau route via the WLD section, national manager ON4CJK and DARC" loading="lazy">
</div>

**Outgoing.** You hand your sorted cards to the **QSL manager of your section (WLD)**. Never hand in directly to the national QSL manager, that is not allowed. The section collects the cards, checks the sorting, and forwards everything monthly to the national QSL manager, **José (ON4CJK)**. He sends the correctly sorted packets to the German sister society **DARC**, which packs everything and ships it worldwide to the IARU bureaus.

**Incoming.** International cards are, for now, sorted within the UBA itself per section and go **11 times per year** to the local managers, and so to you. If a section has fewer than 10 cards ready (about 30 g), that shipment is merged with the next month's.

> ⚠️ **Important change since 2026:** because of the new cooperation with DARC, **correct sorting is now mandatory**. Where an eye was sometimes turned in the past, poorly sorted packets are now **returned unprocessed, at the section's expense**. All the more reason to hand in your cards neatly pre-sorted.

## 6. Sorting, Step by Step {#sorting}

Whoever hands in cards **must sort them beforehand**. That saves your section manager an enormous amount of work and stops a shipment from being refused.

<div class="img-full">
  <img src="/assets/images/qsl-sorting.svg" alt="Six-step QSL sorting workflow plus the rules for sorting on the manager and splitting US calls by digit" loading="lazy">
</div>

1. Lay out all your outgoing cards on the table.
2. Determine the correct **IARU QSL bureau** for each card with the sort list V1.0 (see [section 7](#sortlist)).
3. Group all cards for the same bureau together.
4. Bundle each country packet with an **elastic band**, even if it is just one card.
5. Lay the bundles in **alphabetical** order of the bureau code.
6. Keep aside the cards for countries marked **"no IARU QSL bureau available"**, those cannot go via the bureau (see [section 8](#nobureau)).

## 7. Using the Sort List V1.0 {#sortlist}

The UBA provides two tools for looking up the correct bureau:

- **QBuS** (QSL Bureau Search) at [qbus.uba.be](https://qbus.uba.be/online/qbus-online.php): the official online lookup by Rik ON7YD. Enter any callsign or prefix and it tells you the correct IARU bureau and whether one exists at all. Free, no login needed.
- **Sort list V1.0 PDF**: the printable reference, available for UBA members in the [UBA Downloads area](https://www.uba.be/en/downloads-uba) (login required). It has three columns: the **prefix** (e.g. DL, G, W, ON), the **country or region**, and the **packet** (the bureau code to sort under).

A few worked examples:

- You worked **DL1ABC** (Germany). Prefix "DA to DR" goes in packet **DL**.
- You worked **G3XYZ** (UK). Prefix "G" goes in packet **G**.
- You worked an **ON station**. Prefix "ON to OT" goes in packet **ON** (domestic).

> 🇺🇸 **Special case, the United States:** American calls (K, W, N and AA to AK) are sorted **by the digit in the callsign** (W1 through W0). So **W6XYZ** goes in packet **W6**, not simply in "W".

### Common destinations (excerpt)

A handful of bureaus you will meet most often as a beginner. When in doubt, always confirm with QBuS.

| Prefix | Country / region | Packet |
|---|---|---|
| ON – OT | Belgium | ON |

| DA – DR | Germany | DL |

| I, IA – IZ | Italy | I |
| EA – EH | Spain (incl. islands) | EA |

> 💡 Exotic prefix you don't recognise? QBuS will handle it. Also cross-check with the [IARU list of QSL bureaus](https://www.iaru.org/reference/qsl-bureau-2/) to confirm the bureau is still active.

## 8. Countries Without an IARU Bureau {#nobureau}

Not every country has a working QSL bureau. If a prefix is marked **"no IARU QSL bureau available"**, you cannot send that card via the bureau. You then have two options:

- Is there a **QSL manager** (asked with "QSL via?" during the QSO)? Then sort the card on the manager's bureau.
- Is there no manager? Then send the card **direct** to the given address, with return postage if you want a card back.

> 🧭 A good DX-er always keeps the little list of countries *without* a bureau at hand. The sort list V1.0 indicates this for every prefix.

---

## 9. Receiving Cards {#receiving}

The other direction works by itself, as long as your details are correct. Incoming cards are delivered to you 11 times per year via your section, usually at the monthly section meeting. The sorting system works on the basis of the membership database, so if your callsign is not in there (correctly), you cannot receive cards. Keep your details current via [members@uba.be](mailto:members@uba.be).

## 10. Checklist for Your First Batch {#checklist}

Run through this list before you hand your cards in:

- [ ] I am a UBA member and affiliated with my section (WLD / ON6WL).
- [ ] My callsign (and any extra calls) is correct in the membership administration.
- [ ] Every card carries: counterpart call, date, UTC time, band, mode and RST report.
- [ ] The addressee's callsign is prominent, and the bureau address is not on the card.
- [ ] "VIA manager" is correctly noted where needed (and sorted on the right bureau).
- [ ] All cards are sorted per IARU bureau with the sort list V1.0.
- [ ] The country packets are bundled with an elastic band and ordered alphabetically.
- [ ] Cards for countries without a bureau are kept aside (direct or via manager).
- [ ] I hand everything to the QSL manager of my section, not directly to the national manager.

## 11. What Changed in 2026 {#changes2026}

In CQ-QSO 05-06/2026 the board explained a thorough reorganisation of the QSL service. In short:

- The Dutch sheltered workshop **Scalabor**, which sorted and packed the cards for years (also for VERON), stopped that task after an internal restructuring, a retirement and a relocation.
- Outgoing cards now run via the national QSL manager **José (ON4CJK)** to the **DARC**, which takes over the packing and worldwide shipping.
- Incoming cards are, for now, sorted **within the UBA itself** per section, with a new self-built system that already uses optical callsign recognition.
- For the individual member little changes in practice, **except that correct sorting is now strictly enforced**.

> ℹ️ The service therefore simply continues for all 3,000-plus members. Rumours of a phase-out are unfounded.

## 12. A Few Things I Learned Along the Way {#learned}

These are not in my source bundle, but they are the lessons that saved me effort. Treat them as a fellow newcomer's notes, not gospel:

- **Check the QSL policy before you send a bureau card.** Look the station up on QRZ.com. Many DX stations and DXpeditions are "LoTW only" or "direct / OQRS only", and a bureau card to them is wasted effort. If someone says "no QSL needed, LoTW only", respect it.
- **The bureau is slow on purpose, so match the tool to the goal.** For an everyday European contact, the bureau is perfect. For a new DXCC entity you are proud of and want quickly, direct mail or Club Log OQRS is faster.
- **A bureau card still needs both halves.** You send yours, they send theirs. If the other side does not work the bureau, your card may never be answered. That is not rudeness, just a different workflow.
- **Paper and digital are not rivals.** I log everything digitally (LoTW and eQSL) for the awards, and send paper through the bureau for the ones I want on the wall. The two tracks live happily side by side.
